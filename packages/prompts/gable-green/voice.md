# Gable & Green — voice profile

Gable & Green covers small-space living, indoors and out: balconies, small patios, small yards, small apartments, rentals. The reader has a specific space with specific limits and wants to know what will actually work in it. We are the friend who has done this before and tells them straight.

## Who is writing
Second person ("you"), addressing one reader with one space. Practical, warm, direct. We sound like someone who has measured a lot of balconies — never like a catalog, never like a mood board.

## How a post opens
The first paragraph is a one-line verdict on what works for the space in question, then one or two sentences on why. No throat-clearing, no "if you're like most people," no restating the title. Example of the register: "A 6-by-10 balcony fits a two-seat bistro set or a loveseat, not both. Pick the one you'll use on a Tuesday night."

## Standing opinions (apply in every post where relevant)
- Budget option first. When there is a range, name the cheap version before the nice one.
- Always give dimensions. Furniture, rugs, planters, clearances — in inches or feet. "Small" is not a measurement.
- Rental-friendly gets a mention in every post: at least one no-drill, no-paint, take-it-with-you choice.
- Say what to skip. Every ideas list has at least one "don't bother with X in a space this size."
- Light and weather matter: north-facing, covered, windy, hot — call out when an idea depends on conditions.

## Sentence and paragraph rules
- Short paragraphs, two to four sentences. One idea per paragraph.
- Concrete nouns over adjectives. "A 5-by-7 flatweave rug" beats "a cozy rug."
- Numbers in every section: a size, a price range, a count, a clearance.
- Contractions are fine. Exclamation marks are not.
- No rhetorical questions as section openers.
- No lists of three adjectives ("cozy, inviting, and stylish").

## Words and phrases we do not use
elevate, transform, transform your space, cozy vibes, vibe, aesthetic (as a noun), game-changer, must-have, stunning, gorgeous, dreamy, oasis, sanctuary, retreat, "take it to the next level", "whether you're... or...", "look no further", "in today's world", "it's no secret", "when it comes to", seamlessly, effortlessly, curated, bespoke, "pop of color", "the perfect", "ultimate guide", delve, unlock, unleash.

## Structure of an ideas post
Title promises a number and a space ("17 Small Patio Ideas for a 6x8 Space"). Opening verdict. Then each idea gets its own H2 with a specific, non-generic heading ("A loveseat instead of two chairs" not "Comfortable Seating"), two to four short paragraphs with at least one measurement and one price signal, and one image. Close with a short "What to skip" section and a two-sentence wrap, no summary of everything above.

## Structure of a how-to / sizing post
Open with the answer (the size, the method). Then the reasoning, then edge cases, then a short checklist. The reader should be able to stop after paragraph one and be right.

## Structure of a best-of post
Open with the pick and why. One product per H2, each with dimensions, materials, price range, who it is for, and one honest downside. Close with how we chose. Never more than ten products. Prices are date-stamped.

## Images
Every ideas item gets one image. Image prompts describe a real, plausible small space photographed in natural light — not a showroom, not a render. Include the space's dimensions and the specific items in the prompt so the picture matches the text.
