# Keyword Opportunity Map and Launch Wedges: Small-Space Living (Indoors + Out), US

Research date: 2026-11-09. Method: (a) public keyword-volume pages that expose Google Ads API averages (seodata.dev, Treendly, RankHero), (b) Pinterest's own trend reports, (c) secondhand Google Trends seasonality write-ups, and (d) direct SERP observation of ~25 representative queries via web search (top ~15-25 organic results per query). Reliability notes are inline. All volumes are US monthly averages unless stated, and all are estimates: Google Ads API volumes are bucketed (e.g., 2,900 / 3,600 / 4,400 / 5,400 / 6,600 / 8,100 / 9,900 / 12,100 / 14,800) and can be off by 2x in either direction. SERP observations came from a general web-search tool, not a logged-out US Google SERP capture; treat domain lists as "who is competing on page 1-2," not exact rank order.

## Key Question 1: What are the highest-demand query families in this niche, with US volume estimates?

### Takeaway
The niche has two 10K-class outdoor heads ("small patio ideas" ~9.9K-12.1K and "small backyard ideas" ~9.9K), one 15K-class adjacent head that is not really small-space ("covered patio ideas" ~14.8K, "patio covering ideas" ~18.1K), a 3-5K balcony cluster that fragments into many 2-4K variants, and a 4-5K indoor off-season cluster around "studio apartment layout." Sizing, constraint-modifier, and best-of queries are individually small (hundreds, not thousands) but are where the SERPs are weakest.

### Inferences
- Total addressable "ideas" demand for the outdoor small-space core (small patio + small balcony + apartment balcony + small backyard + no-grass variants) is roughly 35-50K US searches/month across the bucketed variants, before counting long-tail. The balcony cluster alone is ~15-20K when you sum the 2-4K variants, which matters because Google treats most of them as one intent (seodata.dev shows 20/20 SERP overlap between "small balcony ideas," "small balcony decor ideas," and "small balcony design ideas"), so one strong page can capture the whole set.
- "Covered patio ideas" is big (14.8K) but has contractor-grade CPC ($1.46-$3.34) and a SERP full of decking/pergola brands and homebuilders; it is a renovation query, not a small-space query. Treat it as adjacent, not core.
- The "studio apartment layout" cluster is the best indoor off-season anchor found: ~4-5K per variant, near-zero advertiser competition (0.02), and content that is evergreen (floor plans by square footage).
- Pinterest's growth stats are directional only (no baseline given), but the consistent pattern (balcony makeover, small-space reading chair, small-space laundry organization, budget patio) confirms the small-space + renter framing is rising rather than fading.

- Pinterest Trends tool values not retrievable without login; only newsroom growth stats were available.

### Inferences
- A reliable thinness signal in this niche is the presence of coohom.com (a 3D-design SaaS publishing templated "5 X ideas that actually work" posts, often under its /in/ India path). It appeared on narrow balcony, no-drill, balcony privacy, umbrella sizing, string lights, north-facing, and apartment balcony. Where a templated SaaS blog ranks, a genuinely original US page with measurements and photos should displace it.
- Future plc (Homes & Gardens, Livingetc, Real Homes, Gardeningetc, Ideal Home) is the dominant publisher across the whole outdoor niche, but their pages are UK-written and generic (no US sizing, no renter/HOA framing). That is the opening: US-specific, measurement-specific, renter-specific.
- Retailer "ideas" hubs (Home Depot /c/ai/, Wayfair, Pottery Barn, West Elm, Lowe's) are thin and product-list heavy; they rank on domain authority, not content. They are vulnerable on long-tail but unbeatable on heads in year one.
- Sizing SERPs are 100% retailer buying guides with no editorial authority; a neutral "measure this, buy that size" page with diagrams fits an unoccupied slot.
